## 1. Multi-Factor Damage Assessment Engine

The Damage Assessment Engine (`DamageAssessmentEngine`) analyzes pipeline assets by pulling diagnostic telemetry across five distinct sub-modules:
1. **Task 04 GIS Pipeline Intelligence**: Real pipe diameter ($D_{\text{mm}}$), pipe wall material, installation year/age, and nominal service pressure ($P_{\text{nominal}}$).
2. **Task 07 AI Acoustic Intelligence**: Hydro-acoustic anomaly signatures, acoustic decibel confidence, and frequency domain leak indicators.
3. **Task 09 Predictive Maintenance**: Remaining useful life (RUL), asset health index ($H \in [0, 100]$), and failure probability in 7 days ($P_{\text{fail, 7d}}$).
4. **Task 11 Infrastructure Intelligence**: Spatial vulnerability score ($V_{\text{vuln}} \in [0, 100]$) and DBSCAN incident clustering.
5. **Task 15 Autonomous Inspection Rovers**: Video crawler visual defect logs, in-pipe caliper anomalies, and leak severity tags.

### 1.1 Damage Categories (8 Enumerated Classifications)

| Classification | Diagnostic Criteria | Typical Structural Mechanism |
| :--- | :--- | :--- |
| `PIPE_LEAK` | Acoustic frequency peak in leak band (100–1200 Hz), positive acoustic confidence, leak loss $0 < Q_{\text{leak}} < 500$ L/h. | Longitudinal pinhole, localized pit corrosion or minor hairline joint seep. |
| `PIPE_BURST` | Burst risk $\ge 0.25$, sudden dynamic pressure drop, or estimated water loss $Q_{\text{leak}} \ge 500$ L/h. | Full circumferential rupture, catastrophic shear blowout, or major longitudinal split. |
| `JOINT_FAILURE` | Anomaly localized within 0.5m of pipe bell/spigot, or bell joint displacement detected by rover inspection. | Gasket pullout, degraded elastomeric seal, or thermal contraction dislodgement. |
| `CORROSION` | High vulnerability score ($V_{\text{vuln}} \ge 70$), degraded health ($H < 50$), metallic pipe (Ductile/Cast/Steel). | Severe external electrochemical pitting or internal tuberculation thinning pipe wall. |
| `STRUCTURAL_CRACK` | Pipe health severely compromised ($H < 40$), shear or beam stress indicator detected. | Beam bending under soil settlement, traffic overburden, or frost heave fracture. |
| `PRESSURE_DAMAGE` | High nominal pressure ($P_{\text{nominal}} \ge 80$ PSI), elevated surge risk ($P_{\text{fail, 7d}} > 0.15$). | Water hammer cyclic fatigue, transient cavitation erosion, or hoop stress yield. |
| `VALVE_FAILURE` | Isolation or PRV assembly binding, seating leakage, or actuator refusal during rover acoustic survey. | Worn resilient seat, seized stem threads, or eroded diaphragm guide. |
| `UNKNOWN_DAMAGE` | Uncorrelated sensor readings, contradictory evidence, or lack of direct asset records. | Ambiguous subsurface anomaly requiring physical verification or secondary crawler. |

### 1.2 Severity Tiers (5 Levels)

- **`CRITICAL`**: Active rupture ($Q_{\text{leak}} \ge 400$ L/h), pipe burst category, asset health index $H < 30$, or loss of supply to primary arterial zone. Requires 24-hour emergency intervention.
- **`HIGH`**: Significant leakage ($150 \le Q_{\text{leak}} < 400$ L/h), severe vulnerability ($V_{\text{vuln}} \ge 70$), or health index $H < 50$. Requires 72-hour expedited repair.
- **`MODERATE`**: Measurable leakage ($30 \le Q_{\text{leak}} < 150$ L/h) or structural vulnerability $45 \le V_{\text{vuln}} < 70$. Managed under 14-day planned maintenance.
- **`LOW`**: Superficial defect ($Q_{\text{leak}} < 30$ L/h) or early stage corrosion with sound remaining wall thickness. Managed under 30-day routine scheduling.
- **`UNDETERMINED`**: Missing critical baseline telemetry ($\ge 3$ missing parameters). System conservatively flags for mandatory professional engineering review.

## 2. Six-Tier Ontological Evidence Classification

To prevent algorithmic hallucination and ensure absolute transparency, every diagnostic observation, metric, and finding ingested into a damage assessment is tagged with strict 6-tier ontological provenance:

```
[MEASURED]           Empirically captured by physical sensors (caliper, pressure transducer, flowmeter).
[SIMULATED]          Generated by hydraulic solver equations (WNTR/EPANET pressure curves).
[DERIVED]            Deterministically calculated via engineering formulas (contingencies, Haversine distance).
[PREDICTED]          Inferred via probabilistic ML/AI models (random forest RUL, acoustic leak classifier).
[OPERATOR_ENTERED]   Manually recorded by a certified field engineer or operations manager.
[UNKNOWN]            Unverified legacy records or missing metadata sources.
```

> [!IMPORTANT]
> **Strict AI Provenance Rule**: Under no circumstances does the system convert a `PREDICTED` machine learning score (such as an acoustic classifier output) into a confirmed physical diagnosis. AI predictions remain categorized as `PREDICTED` until reconciled with physical sensor measurements (`MEASURED`) or certified operator inspection (`OPERATOR_ENTERED`).

### Evidence Completeness Formulation

Evidence completeness ($C_{\text{evid}} \in [20\%, 100\%]$) measures the diagnostic completeness of available data:

$$C_{\text{evid}} = \max\left(20.0\%, \; 100.0\% - (N_{\text{missing}} \times 20.0\%)\right)$$

Where $N_{\text{missing}}$ represents the count of absent diagnostic vectors:
- Direct GIS pipeline metadata record
- Task 15 autonomous rover inspection log
- Task 07 acoustic frequency survey
- Task 09 predictive RUL/health telemetry

When $C_{\text{evid}} < 50.0\%$, the Repair Method Engine automatically enforces fallback to `FURTHER_INSPECTION_REQUIRED`.

## 3. Repair Prioritization Engine Formulation

Repair prioritization is calculated via a dedicated multi-factor composite equation, specifically distinct from the inspection prioritization engine developed in Task 15. While inspection priority focuses on sensor coverage and anomaly discovery, repair priority focuses on **water asset conservation, public safety, and hydraulic infrastructure resilience**.

### 3.1 Mathematical Formulation

The composite repair priority score ($S_{\text{repair}} \in [0, 100]$) is computed as:

$$S_{\text{repair}} = \sum_{k \in \mathcal{K}} w_k \cdot f_k$$

Where the factor weights ($w_k$) satisfy $\sum w_k = 1.0$:

$$\begin{aligned}
w_{\text{severity}} &= 0.30 \quad (30\%) \\
w_{\text{water\_loss}} &= 0.25 \quad (25\%) \\
w_{\text{criticality}} &= 0.20 \quad (20\%) \\
w_{\text{vulnerability}} &= 0.15 \quad (15\%) \\
w_{\text{hotspot}} &= 0.10 \quad (10\%)
\end{aligned}$$

### 3.2 Individual Contributing Factor Functions ($f_k$)

1. **Damage Severity Factor ($f_{\text{severity}} \in [20, 100]$)**:
   $$f_{\text{severity}} = \begin{cases} 
   100.0 & \text{if Severity} = \text{CRITICAL} \\
   75.0 & \text{if Severity} = \text{HIGH} \\
   50.0 & \text{if Severity} = \text{MODERATE} \\
   25.0 & \text{if Severity} = \text{LOW} \\
   20.0 & \text{if Severity} = \text{UNDETERMINED}
   \end{cases}$$

2. **Water Loss Magnitude Factor ($f_{\text{water\_loss}} \in [0, 100]$)**:
   Scales linearly up to catastrophic rupture capacity ($500\text{ L/h}$):
   $$f_{\text{water\_loss}} = \min\left(100.0, \; \frac{Q_{\text{leak, lph}}}{500.0} \times 100.0\right)$$

3. **Conduit Criticality & Diameter Factor ($f_{\text{criticality}} \in [0, 100]$)**:
   Reflects the hydraulic importance of arterial transmission mains ($D_{\text{mm}} \ge 600\text{ mm}$):
   $$f_{\text{criticality}} = \min\left(100.0, \; \frac{D_{\text{mm}}}{600.0} \times 100.0\right)$$

4. **Structural Vulnerability Factor ($f_{\text{vulnerability}} \in [0, 100]$)**:
   Directly derived from the Task 11 geospatial pipe degradation score:
   $$f_{\text{vulnerability}} = V_{\text{vuln}}$$

5. **Spatial Hotspot Risk Factor ($f_{\text{hotspot}} \in [20, 85]$)**:
   Reflects whether the asset resides within an active DBSCAN spatial incident cluster:
   $$f_{\text{hotspot}} = \begin{cases} 
   85.0 & \text{if asset inside active incident cluster} \\
   20.0 & \text{otherwise}
   \end{cases}$$
